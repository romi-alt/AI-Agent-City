  Executive summary
  We propose turning the single Fast Sales virtual office into AgentCity (working name): a
  multi-tenant platform where an agency runs AI-staffed offices for many clients, all shown
  as one living 3D city. Each client gets a building, each team a floor, and each AI agent a
  desk. Behind them are real CRM data, real outbound messages and a full audit trail.

  The pilot already proves the core loop. Seven agents work a live GoHighLevel account:
  they answer new leads in under a minute, follow up, report daily, and stay grounded in an
  871-chunk course knowledge base. A 3D office shows their work live. What is missing is
  the layer that makes this repeatable: tenant isolation, self-serve onboarding, a shared
  agent runtime, billing, and white-label controls.

  The ask is a phased build. Phase 1 hardens the pilot into a three-tenant beta. Phase 2 adds
  the city and the agency console. Phase 3 opens a marketplace of agents and integrations.
  Each phase ends at a gate tied to paying clients, not features.

  The vision: a living city of AI-run offices
  The city is both the product's map and its control room. An agency owner sees every
  client as a building on the skyline: lit windows show agents at work, and a red beacon flags
  a building that needs a human. Zooming in moves from district to building to floor to the
  desk where an agent is texting a lead right now.

  Platform architecture
  One shared platform serves every tenant. Isolation comes from identity and the data
  layer, not from separate copies per client. That keeps upgrades to a single release while
  each client's data stays sealed.

  platform architecture · 5 layers

  The agent runtime is the heart of the platform. Each agent run is a durable workflow: it
  reads CRM and knowledge context, asks the LLM gateway for a draft, checks its
  guardrails, then sends through a connector or waits for a human approval. Every step
  emits a live event, and that is what moves the characters in the city.

  Security, data isolation and compliance
  No client can ever see, or be messaged by, another client's agents. The design enforces
  this at four levels.




                                                                                                              Page 5 of 8
AI Agent City — Multi-Client Platform Proposal




    1. Tenant isolation. Every row carries a tenant id, and PostgreSQL row-level security
        enforces it in the database, not just the code. Each tenant's CRM tokens sit in a secrets
        vault under their own encryption key.
    2. Access. Roles cascade: agency owner, agency staff, client admin, client viewer. Sign-in
       uses SSO or passkeys with email codes. Sensitive changes need step-up confirmation,
        and sessions are bound to the device.
    3. Agent guardrails. Agents send only through approved channels and templates. They
       respect DND and quiet hours, and they cap messages per lead per day. Anything
        outside policy goes to a human approval queue.
    4. Audit. Every agent decision, prompt, model, message and approval is logged and can
        be replayed for review.

  Compliance targets:

        US texting rules: A2P 10DLC registration per client, opt-out handling and TCPA consent
        records.
        Email: CAN-SPAM, plus SPF, DKIM and DMARC per sending domain.
        Privacy: GDPR and CCPA data export and deletion per contact.
        Certification: SOC 2 Type I readiness by the end of Phase 2, Type II after.


  Agency model: white-label, onboarding, pricing
  The agency resells AgentCity under its own brand and earns recurring revenue per client
  building. Clients see the agency's logo, domain and colours, never ours.

  Onboarding in under one day per client:

    1. Pick an industry template: training, auto, real estate, home services or clinics.
    2. Connect the CRM with a one-click OAuth or private token.
    3. Upload the client's material (websites, PDFs, FAQs) to build their knowledge base.
    4. Hire agents from the catalogue and set tone, hours and approval rules.
    5. Run a 48-hour shadow mode where agents draft and humans approve, then go live.
